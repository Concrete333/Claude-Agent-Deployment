# Claude-native prose trial, A and D — 10 September 2026

**The forced-worker workflow cost 20.6% less than solo and passed the same checks, but the orchestrator did no substantive verification.** One Opus 5 `low` worker read the 50 discussions, wrote the answer and passed the supplied checker; the Fable 5.1 `medium` orchestrator read the skill and the task, dispatched once, re-ran the checker and finished in five turns. Blind review found the two answers equally faithful: no material error in either, one completeness omission and two wording issues each. One run per arm; this is a pilot, not a policy result.

## What each arm did

A read `TASK.md`, `check.py` and all 50 threads one file at a time (52 Read calls), wrote `answer.json` once, ran the checker and reported. Output was 22,975 tokens; cache writes 87,104; cache reads 300,893.

D's orchestrator read `SKILL.md` and `TASK.md`, dispatched one `scout` Agent call, ran `python -B check.py .` itself and reported. It did not open any thread or the answer. Its final message relayed five records the worker had flagged as judgment calls (ISSUE-013, 015, 025, 026, 030) as "worth a spot-check if decision accuracy matters", without checking them. All five are correct in the answer. The worker (transcript confirms `claude-opus-5`, effort `low`) read the task, the checker and all 50 threads, wrote the answer once, ran the checker, and reported complete.

## Reading the result against the Codex evidence

This is the workflow the Codex team could not get: writer and reader both moved to the cheap worker, and the coordinator did not reread. It is cheaper for exactly that reason. It is also the workflow whose accuracy the Codex diagnostics warned about: their Luna initial answer contained a meaning reversal that only a full coordinator reread caught, and two cheaper reviewers with full coverage missed it. Here there was no reversal to catch and nobody looked. One clean sample cannot distinguish "Opus 5 low is reliable on this task" from "this run happened to be clean". The Codex-side Opus 5 low builder diagnostic on the same corpus had a wording error (ISSUE-015) and five invalid quotes; this run had neither. The supplied checker is the likely reason for the quotes: the worker ran it and passed, rather than writing its own looser check.

The skill's new sentence, "if acceptance requires you to read the same sources the worker reads, delegating the writing does not reduce cost", did not bind here because the forced directive told the orchestrator to delegate and "retain final verification", and the orchestrator satisfied that with the mechanical checker alone. The skill does not say what final verification of a source-fidelity deliverable consists of. That is the gap to close before a C arm can be interpreted.

Both answers were better than the Codex A/C answers on ISSUE-016 (failover eligibility stated correctly) and matched the Codex D answer. The 29/50 and 31/50 author-anchor figures are lower than the Codex runs' 47/50 and 36/50; that diagnostic is not a score, and the blind review found the alternative passages sufficient.

## Harness findings worth keeping

- `--safe-mode` silently disables custom agents passed with `--agents`. In that state the orchestrator, told to use `scout`, substituted the built-in Explore agent (which ran Opus 5 at the inherited `medium` effort) instead of reporting a blocker. That is the substitution the skill forbids, observed in the wild.
- Under `--permission-mode dontAsk` with no `--allowedTools`, every Write, Bash and PowerShell call is denied. Attempt 2 of A read everything and drafted a complete answer ($3.33, 59 turns) that it could not save. The draft, recovered from the denied Write call, also passes the grader (50/50, 50/50, 32 anchors) and the checker; it is retained privately as a third Fable data point, not as an arm.
- Default cache writes were one-hour; `FORCE_PROMPT_CACHING_5M=1` was applied and confirmed in every receipt.
- Per-model usage and cost are in the `modelUsage` block of the JSON result; per-worker model and effort are in the subagent transcript under `~/.claude/projects/<cwd>/<session>/subagents/`. Effort was verified for the worker, not requested-only.
- The user's global `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` (distill instructions) was loaded in every arm because safe mode could not be used. The prompt forbade distill; neither arm used it.

## Spend

Trial sessions: A $2.3157, D $1.8386. Overhead: four smoke sessions $1.15 (a budget-exceeded first turn, two safe-mode failures, one success), six Haiku probes about $0.16, the denied-tools A attempt $3.33. Total about $8.80 API-equivalent, excluding this evaluation. The $8-per-session cap was never reached.

## Limits

One run per arm, uncontrolled sampling and cache state, a corpus already used in five prior runs and known to the evaluator, model-based blind review (labels shuffled, reviewer given only sources and answers), no C arm, no repair pass (none was needed under the contract as reviewed). The 20% saving is one sample on a task shaped to favour context-isolating delegation; it does not transfer to coupled implementation work or to tasks where the worker's error rate is non-zero.

## Next

Stage 1 of the trial plan is still the right next step: give Opus 4.8 `max`, Fable 5.1 `high` and Opus 5 `low` the saved Luna initial answer as reviewers and see who catches ISSUE-040. Then a C arm on this corpus, with the skill amended to say what final verification of a source-fidelity deliverable means.
